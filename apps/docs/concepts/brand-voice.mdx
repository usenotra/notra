Brand voice controls how generated content sounds. In Notra it lives in **Brand Identity**. Every generation path picks one identity and writes with it: the Create Content wizard, schedules, event triggers and GEO Write.

## What is a brand identity?

A brand identity is a named profile. A workspace starts with one called **Default**, created from the website you entered during onboarding. You can add more, for example Marketing, Technical or Internal. Each identity stores:

* **Company name** is your product or company name.
* **Website** is the site Notra analyzes for the profile, sitemap and guidelines.
* **Description** says what you build and why it matters.
* **Tone profile** is Conversational, Professional, Casual or Formal.
* **Custom tone notes** are free-text guidance layered on top of the profile.
* **Language** is the language content is written in.
* **Custom instructions** are rules the writer must follow.
* **Who are you writing for?** describes your target audience.

The Brand Identity page has four tabs: **Company Info** (the fields above), **References**, **Sitemap** and **Brand Guidelines**.

## How Notra learns your brand

### Automated analysis

When you add a website, Notra runs a three-step analysis and fills in Company Info for you:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Scraping">
    Notra fetches your homepage and key pages and extracts the main content.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Extracting">
    An AI model reads the content and identifies your company name, description, tone, style patterns and the audience your messaging speaks to.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Saving">
    Notra writes the results into the identity. Review and adjust any field afterwards.
  </Step>
</Steps>

During onboarding this runs in the background right after you create the workspace. You can re-run it on the Brand Identity page whenever your site changes. Adding a new identity with a website also runs it for that identity.

### Manual configuration

Every field is editable. Changes save a moment after you stop typing, and a toast confirms the update.

### References

The **References** tab holds real posts and writing samples, for example tweets, that help the AI match your style beyond what a tone profile can express. Add a few pieces you are proud of. The number of references included depends on your plan.

### Sitemap and Brand Guidelines

The **Sitemap** tab tracks your indexed pages and monitors site health for AI discovery. GEO Write uses it to pick internal links for new articles.

The **Brand Guidelines** tab pulls logos, colors, typography and landing page screenshots from your website. Generate them once, refresh when your design changes and edit individual entries.

## Tone profiles

Each tone profile selects a different prompt template for every format.

### Conversational

Best for developer tools, startups and open source projects.

* Warm and authentic
* Direct address ("we" and "you")
* Clear and specific without being dry
* Founder-to-community feel

Example:

> We've shipped some meaningful improvements this week. The new cache component support catches unsupported auth calls early and provides clear migration guidance. Email verification flows now support secure link completion with proper error handling for expired links.

### Professional

Best for enterprise products, B2B SaaS and corporate communications.

* Clear and confident
* Polished without being stiff
* Focus on business value

Example:

> This release delivers significant enhancements to the platform. New cache component validation ensures proper auth implementation with actionable error guidance. Email verification workflows now include comprehensive link-based completion flows.

### Casual

Best for consumer apps, community-driven products and creative tools.

* Friendly and approachable
* Relaxed language
* Enthusiasm visible

Example:

> Hey folks! We've been busy this week. The cache system is now way smarter about catching auth issues before they become problems. Plus, email verification links actually work the way you'd expect, nice and reliable.

### Formal

Best for finance, healthcare, compliance-heavy industries and academic tools.

* Precise and structured
* Traditional language
* Complete sentences
* Conservative terminology

Example:

> The engineering team has completed several deliverables for this release cycle. Runtime validation capabilities have been enhanced to detect unsupported authentication patterns in cached contexts. Email-based verification procedures have been implemented with comprehensive error state handling.

<Tip>
  **Custom tone notes** refine the profile without replacing it. "Technical but enthusiastic about developer experience" on top of Conversational works better than trying to describe the whole voice from scratch.
</Tip>

## Language

The **Language** field tells the writer which language to produce. The rule is strict. The final text is written primarily in that language. English appears only for terms the language commonly borrows, such as technical terms and product names. This applies even when your custom instructions and references are in English.

## Target audience

**Who are you writing for?** changes both filtering and formatting.

### Developer audience

When the audience is developers, engineers or technical teams:

* Every referenced change links to its pull request when one is available.
* The writer uses proper technical terms without over-explaining.
* Implementation details such as APIs, components and architectural changes are fair game.
* The writer credits contributors with their GitHub handle.

Example:

> **Cache component support with actionable error guidance** [#423](https://github.com/org/repo/pull/423) - Runtime guardrails now catch unsupported auth calls in cached contexts and provide clear migration guidance with the correct usage pattern. (Author: [@alex](https://github.com/alex/))

### Non-developer audience

When the audience is customers, end users or stakeholders:

* No PR numbers or links appear anywhere in the output.
* Plain language explains what changed in terms of benefits.
* The text focuses on outcomes, meaning what users can now do.
* Author attribution is still included.

Example:

> **Improved error messages**: When you use authentication features incorrectly, you'll now see helpful guidance on how to fix it. (Author: [@alex](https://github.com/alex/))

The audience also decides what gets filtered out. For a customer audience, Notra drops a change that only matters to engineers instead of translating it into vague copy.

## Custom instructions

Custom instructions add rules beyond the tone profile:

```
Avoid cryptocurrency and blockchain jargon
Emphasize security and compliance improvements
Include performance metrics when available
Mention customer impact explicitly for each feature
Use "we" instead of "the team" or third person
```

<Note>
  Be specific. "Use simple language" is vague. "Explain technical concepts using analogies from everyday life" gives the writer a rule it can follow.
</Note>

## How brand voice is applied

When Notra generates content, it formats the identity into structured tags and places them in the prompt:

```xml
<company>Acme Corp - Building developer tools for modern web apps</company>
<target-audience>Software developers and engineering teams</target-audience>
<language>English</language>
<custom-instructions>
Keep technical accuracy while being approachable
Emphasize security and performance improvements
</custom-instructions>

<background-data>
<sources>github:acme/platform</sources>
<today-utc>2026-09-02</today-utc>
</background-data>
```

Two rules travel with these tags:

1. `<company>` is the publishing identity. Sources are evidence only, so a repository, workspace or team with a different name never causes the writer to hesitate or attribute the work elsewhere.
2. If `<language>` is present, the final text is written in that language.

The tone-specific system prompt for the chosen format wraps all of this. Any skills you enabled on the Skills page, such as the human-writer skill, run on the result.

## Multiple identities

Use separate identities when one voice does not fit every channel:

* **Marketing** for blog posts and LinkedIn, Conversational, customer audience
* **Technical** for changelogs, Professional, developer audience
* **Internal** for team updates, Casual

The Create Content wizard, schedules, event triggers and GEO Write each let you pick the identity for that run. One identity is the default. Notra uses it when you select nothing else.

## Consistency

A changelog and the tweet announcing it come from the same identity. Scheduled, event-triggered and manual runs all use the same rules.

Changelogs always use the sections Summary, Highlights and More Updates, with categories in a fixed priority order.

Quality controls:

* Summaries target 120 to 180 words
* No made-up PR numbers or commit details
* Runs with nothing audience-relevant are skipped instead of padded

## Updating your brand voice

Update an identity whenever you rebrand, your audience shifts or you want to try a different tone. Future runs use the new settings. Existing drafts keep the voice they were generated with.

<Tip>
  After changing an identity, generate one post from the Create Content wizard to check the result before your schedules run with it.
</Tip>

## Best practices

**Tone.** Match your existing marketing and documentation. Fintech tends toward Formal, developer tools toward Conversational. Change it rarely.

**Custom instructions.** Start small and add rules as you notice yourself making the same manual edit twice. Keep them about style, not content.

**Audience.** Be specific. "Senior engineering leaders at enterprise companies" beats "technical people", and it decides whether PR links appear.

**References.** Add real posts. Three good samples do more for style than a paragraph of description.

## Troubleshooting voice issues

**Too technical or not technical enough.** Adjust the audience description first, then add a custom instruction about detail level.

**Wrong tone.** Check the profile, then make sure custom tone notes and custom instructions do not contradict it.

**Wrong language.** Set the Language field. Instructions written in English do not switch the output language.

**Inconsistent style across posts.** Make sure every schedule and trigger points at the same identity and that the identity has all fields filled in.
