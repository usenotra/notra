Part of why [Vercel](https://vercel.com/) stays top of mind is that many employees regularly share what they are building on Twitter and engage with users and customers. We see the same pattern at [**Anomaly**](https://anoma.ly/) (behind OpenCode), which has hired engineers like [Ryan Vogel](https://x.com/ryanvogel), [Kit Langton](https://x.com/kitlangton) and [Rhys Sullivan](https://x.com/RhysSullivan) who talk about their work in public, and even at larger companies like [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/), where engineers such as [Dillon Mulroy](https://x.com/dillon_mulroy) stream on Twitch and regularly post updates publicly on Twitter.

When people *see* the work happen, they feel closer to the company and the product. They learn why decisions were made, follow the tradeoffs and feel involved in how the product grows.

**Sharing your work in public helps in three ways:**

- It builds trust. When you share progress, you also share your process.
- It shortens feedback loops. Users reply with context, edge cases and ideas while you are still building.
- It makes distribution feel natural, because you are documenting your work instead of writing ads.

### Why this trend accelerates with AI [#why-this-trend-accelerates-with-ai]

AI lets teams ship faster, so more companies will want to turn that shipping speed into distribution. That makes engineers an underrated distribution channel.

**If you are building a developer product, you already have the raw material for great marketing:**

- PRs and shipped features
- interesting implementation details
- benchmarks and performance wins
- the small but real customer problems you solved

Most teams don't have time to turn all of that into content on a regular schedule.

Notra takes the work you already do in GitHub or Linear and turns it into on-brand updates you can publish anywhere. We want to help people talk about the work they and their agents do every day, without adding more filler to the internet.
